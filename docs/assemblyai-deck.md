# Beacon Night Shift — pitch deck (10 slides)

Source for the `.pptx` / PDF. Structure follows lablab's guidance (problem, solution, demo, market, revenue, team, next steps) with the technical proof folded into slides 5–7. The speaker line is what is said, not what is written. Every number was measured on the live AWS account on 21–22 Sep 2026 or is sourced in [`business-case.md`](business-case.md).

| # | Title | On the slide | Speaker line (≤ 20 s) |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | **The on-call agent you can interrupt** | Cover: the Night Board mid-approval. One line: *"A voice agent whose most important sentence is a tool call that can change production."* Name, links, the judge passcode. | Every voice-agent demo is a receptionist. This one holds write access to AWS, and the transcript is the safety artifact. |

Three numbers: **53 min** industry MTTR, improved only 12 % in five years against 3× monitoring spend · **74 %** of DevOps engineers report burnout, on-call load the leading indicator · **$5,600/min** Gartner baseline for downtime. | A four-person team in Bengaluru runs production for customers in New York. There is no second shift. The pager is one person's phone, and most of what wakes them is mechanical. |

| 5 | **Where AssemblyAI sits** | Diagram: browser ⇄ Voice Agent API (Universal-3 Pro, turn detection, managed LLM, TTS, nine client-side tools) ⇄ `POST /tools/<name>` on the voice Lambda ⇄ the backend (registry, Step Functions, DynamoDB). | 6 | **Consent is the product** | Code strip: `approve_fix` ignores its model-written argument and checks `ctx.transcript`. | 7 | **Three things only a socket can do** | (a) Barge-in withdraws the fix: the interrupted read-back cannot be approved. Test names on the slide. | Interrupting Beacon during a read-back does not just stop the audio, it withdraws the fix. If the call drops, the worst case is that nothing happens. That is the right worst case. |

We are selling to the team of one to five running production for another time zone, where there is nobody to escalate to. |
| 9 | **Revenue, and who we are against** | $29/responder/month · **$2 per verified remediation** — you pay when a fix was applied *and* CloudWatch agreed · $15k/yr self-hosted in the customer's own account. Competitor row: PagerDuty routes it, incident.io coordinates it, SSM automates it blindly, Beacon asks first and records the answer. | Charging per verified fix aligns us with the customer: we earn nothing from noisy alerts, only from nights they did not lose. |

Repo · live URL · passcode · film. | Try it: the live URL with the passcode and a microphone, or `make local` and the whole night plays with no AWS account at all. |

## Production notes

- 16:9, the console's cream/ink editorial theme (EB Garamond headings, Figtree body; lavender for contracts, coral for "needs you"), no bullet walls, one idea per slide.
- Slides 6–7 each carry one real screenshot and the name of the test or harness run that proves the behaviour: `tests/test_voice_tools.py::test_cancel_proposal_withdraws_the_pending_fix`, `tests/test_telegram.py::test_voice_note_is_transcribed_with_confidence_and_gated`, `scripts/dev/assemblyai_audio.py … "!drop"`, `tests/test_fix_pr.py`.
- Slide 8's assumptions stay visible on the slide: judges trust a stated assumption more than a big number.
- Export `.pptx` for the form, PDF as backup; slide 1 doubles as the submission thumbnail.
